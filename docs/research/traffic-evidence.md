# Cooperative traffic: evidence and transfer limits

Research cut: 10 October 2026. Supports [proposal 0.1](../cooperative-traffic-plan.md); does not certify RoadMesh performance.

## How this review was conducted

Parallel Orca reviews covered traffic theory, visual demonstration and BLE/Android feasibility. The coordinator checked primary papers, author/institution copies, official documentation and disconfirming evidence. Firecrawl's paper-search path was unavailable without working authentication, so ordinary web access to primary sources was used. This is a focused engineering literature review, not a systematic review or meta-analysis.

Access labels: **F** = full paper/report text inspected by the coordinator or research worker; **A** = primary abstract/publication record only; **D** = official documentation, standard or dataset page. An accessible abstract does not establish that the full methods were reviewed. Source results are not transferred into RoadMesh benchmarks. All algorithms in the proposal are adaptations requiring evaluation.

## Evidence-to-build decisions

1. **Build the simple slowdown warning before promising phantom-jam classification.** T1/T2 establish dynamics; T12 is closer to the actual human-warning intervention. Distinguish early delivery from causal traffic improvement.
2. **Keep static and dynamic hazards separate.** T10/T11 motivate impact features; a persistent surface record needs different expiry, correction and replay semantics from a brake message.
3. **Use topology and receiving space before global route optimization.** T6–T9 inform local reasoning. Sparse reports cannot identify total queue storage without assumptions. T14 explains a cost of premature routing.
4. **Minimize redundant work, not merely bytes.** N1–N4 motivate suppression, supersession and timely carrying. N5/N6 explain why extra packets are not extra independent evidence.
5. **Measure the whole path.** Observation quality, inference, delivery, presentation, driver response and traffic outcome are separate links. Missing one prevents the corresponding outcome claim.

## Corrections made during synthesis

- An initial worker summary characterized wave-damping evidence as exclusively automated. The coordinator checked Stern's full paper and included its trained-human experiment, plus the separate SafeTrip human-alert study.
- Attractive draft storyboard numbers for radio latency, pothole confidence, speed prescriptions, accident savings and RF attenuation were excluded: none were measured RoadMesh results.
- A newly observed stopped probe is not a moving queue boundary; tail growth needs temporal association and coverage checks.
- GNSS multipath is measurement uncertainty, not synthetic provenance. The current unsigned envelope does not provide cryptographic verification. A foreground service does not prove survival under every OEM power policy.

## Open research questions with a decision attached

| Question | Smallest useful next experiment | Decision it informs |
|---|---|---|
| Can the loaners deliver useful warnings before their deadline under contention? | Timestamped receiver traces across exact payloads, screen states and controlled contact patterns | Message size, bursts, scheduling and advertised operating envelope |
| Can a wave be resolved with noisy, sparse phone observations? | Labeled trajectories with probe thinning and clock/location perturbations | Baseline-only warning versus enabled wave forecast |
| Can phone mounting and surface defects be separated reliably? | Held-out devices/vehicles with smooth roads, humps, handling and labeled defects | Rough-road candidate versus specific pothole classification |
| Can observed queues support spillback warnings without false “clear” states? | Signalized and unsignalized traces with participation changes and known movement truth | JunctionShield scope and abstention rules |
| Will ordinary users understand and act usefully on the message? | Comprehension and response study, followed by a controlled pilot | Wording, timing, repetition and any traffic-benefit claim |
| Which identity assumptions are acceptable for open participation? | Explicit adversarial model plus privacy/enrollment prototype | Permitted trust tiers and source influence |

This review found a strong foundation for a testable cooperative advisory product. It did not find evidence that arbitrary smartphone BLE meshes deterministically prevent all traffic jams or accidents. The valuable next result is a reproducible, measured vertical slice with an honest operating envelope.
